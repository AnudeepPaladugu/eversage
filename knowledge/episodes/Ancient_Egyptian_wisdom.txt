Ancient Egyptian wisdom
Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

An official is told to hear the whole concern
Source: Instruction of Ptahhotep — Teaching on receiving a petitioner
Type: Wisdom instruction
Situation: Someone close to me says I never really listen.
What happened: The instruction advises a person receiving a petitioner to listen patiently to the concern. Being heard is treated as important, not merely reaching a quick decision.
Teaching: Its original setting concerns authority and the treatment of a person bringing a complaint.
Why it fits: This fits a problem of feeling unheard, rather than assuming every complaint is correct.
Application: In a personal conversation, it may help to understand the full concern before defending yourself or giving advice.
Try: Ask the person to finish their account, then check your understanding in one sentence before replying.

A wronged traveller keeps asking for a fair hearing
Source: The Eloquent Peasant — Khunanup’s petitions to Rensi
Type: Literary wisdom narrative
Situation: I have been treated unfairly and struggle to explain what happened.
What happened: A peasant travelling with goods is wronged by a man who takes his property. He brings his complaint to an official, Rensi, and gives a series of appeals for justice. The narrative examines power and the obligation to hear a wronged person.
Teaching: The story makes a particular injury visible through clear and sustained speech.
Why it fits: This fits the difficulty of presenting a grievance clearly.
Application: A useful modern step is to set out what happened and what response you seek. The story is not a promise that persistence always brings a fair outcome.
Try: Write the events in order, keep the relevant records and state the specific response you are asking for.
