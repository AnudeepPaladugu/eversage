Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Two students ask the same question and receive different guidance
Source: Analects — 11.22; Zilu and Ranyou
Type: Teaching dialogue
Situation: Other people tell me to copy the advice that worked for someone else.
What happened: Zilu and Ranyou ask whether to act immediately on what they have learned. Confucius restrains one and urges the other forward. He explains that one tends to rush while the other tends to hold back.
Teaching: The dialogue makes guidance responsive to the person’s actual tendency.
Why it fits: This fits conflicting advice and comparison; it does not offer the same answer to everyone.
Application: Before copying someone else’s next step, ask whether your difficulty is rushing without thought or waiting after enough thought.
Try: Name which tendency is stronger in this decision. Choose one action that balances it.

Confucius is questioned during hardship in Chen
Source: Analects — 15.2
Type: Teaching dialogue
Situation: A setback is making me wonder whether I should abandon my values.
What happened: The group is short of food in Chen. Zilu asks whether a worthy person can also face such difficulty. Confucius distinguishes facing hardship from letting hardship dissolve one’s standards.
Teaching: The conversation rejects the idea that good conduct guarantees an easy life.
Why it fits: This fits pressure to give up a principle after misfortune.
Application: A setback is not proof that your values were pointless. You can reconsider a plan without deciding that every principle must be discarded.
Try: Name one value you want to keep and one practical part of your plan you are willing to change.
