Theravada Buddhism
Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Rahula is taught to review an action
Source: Majjhima Nikaya — MN 61, Ambalatthika-Rahulovada Sutta
Type: Teaching dialogue
Situation: I keep repeating something I know I should change.
What happened: The Buddha asks Rahula what a mirror is for, then teaches him to examine actions before, during and after doing them, including whether they cause harm. Mistakes become occasions for acknowledgment and restraint.
Teaching: The teaching gives a sequence for review, not only an instruction to have more willpower.
Why it fits: This fits a repeated action you want to change.
Application: Look for the moment before the pattern starts and the point where you can interrupt it. Review what happened without hiding the result.
Try: Write your trigger, one interruption you can try and a time to review the attempt.

The Buddha distinguishes two kinds of pain
Source: Samyutta Nikaya — SN 36.6, Sallatha Sutta
Type: Teaching illustration
Situation: A problem happened and I keep adding fears and harsh judgments to it.
What happened: The Buddha compares bodily pain with being struck by an arrow, and the added distress of an untrained response with a second arrow. The illustration distinguishes the event from further reactions around it.
Teaching: The teaching makes a distinction; it is not a reason to blame someone for suffering.
Why it fits: This fits rumination after a setback, rather than claiming that grief or illness is merely an attitude.
Application: Notice which part of the difficulty is happening now and which part is a repeated prediction or self-attack. Both deserve care, but they may need different responses.
Try: Write one fact about the event and one fear you are adding. Choose which needs action and which needs gentle attention.
