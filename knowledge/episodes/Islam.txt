Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Yusuf meets the brothers who wronged him
Source: Quran — Surah Yusuf 12:58–92
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: People close to me hurt me and now want to reconnect.
What happened: Yusuf’s brothers come to Egypt without recognising him. Several encounters follow before he reveals himself. In 12:90–92, the reunion includes recognition of wrong and Yusuf’s response to it.
Teaching: The passage places patience, wrongdoing and forgiveness inside a long and difficult family history.
Why it fits: This fits renewed contact after betrayal, rather than a simple disagreement.
Application: You can think carefully about what reconnection would mean. This story is not an instruction to ignore danger or grant another person immediate access to your life.
Try: Name what you would need for a safe conversation before deciding whether to have one.

Yaqub names his sorrow before Allah
Source: Quran — Surah Yusuf 12:83–87
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: I feel ashamed that I still feel deep sorrow.
What happened: Yaqub grieves the separation from his sons. The passage describes the depth of his sadness and his turning with sorrow toward Allah, while he also tells his sons not to give up their search.
Teaching: Sorrow and hope are present in the same passage; sadness is not treated as proof that faith is absent.
Why it fits: This fits the feeling that sorrow and hope cannot exist together.
Application: You can bring grief honestly into prayer and conversation. Yaqub’s circumstances are not the same as every loss, and the story’s reunion is not promised to each reader.
Try: Put your sorrow into honest words in prayer or with a trusted listener, without forcing yourself to sound cheerful.

Yunus calls for help in distress
Source: Quran — Al-Anbiya 21:87–88; As-Saffat 37:139–148
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: I regret a decision and feel trapped by what followed.
What happened: The Quran describes Yunus in darkness calling to Allah, acknowledging wrong, and being delivered from distress. The fuller account places this within his story as a messenger.
Teaching: The passage connects honest acknowledgment with turning for help, rather than hiding in the mistake.
Why it fits: This fits regret about a choice, not blaming yourself for someone else’s harm.
Application: If you regret something you did, begin with what is true and with support. This is not a claim that every hardship is caused by wrongdoing.
Try: Name the part you can take responsibility for and one person who can help you plan a repair.

Hajar keeps looking for help
Source: Sahih al-Bukhari — Hadith 3364
Type: Hadith narrative
Situation: I feel alone while trying to care for someone who depends on me.
What happened: The hadith describes Hajar searching between Safa and Marwa after the water has run out. She looks for help repeatedly while caring for her child. The account then tells of the water at Zamzam.
Teaching: The narrative includes active searching as well as trust in Allah.
Why it fits: This fits a caregiver facing a concrete need alone. Its source is a hadith, not a Quranic verse.
Application: You do not have to wait silently for help. Looking for a practical route to support is compatible with faith; the miracle is not a promise about a particular modern outcome.
Try: Identify the nearest real source of help and make one clear request about the urgent need.

Musa begins again far from home
Source: Quran — Al-Qasas 28:21–28
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: I have to start again and do not know where help will come from.
What happened: Musa reaches Madyan after fleeing Egypt. At the well he helps two women water their animals, then turns to Allah with a prayer of need. A later invitation leads to shelter and an agreement about work.
Teaching: The account joins honest need, useful action and help received through other people.
Why it fits: This fits beginning again after losing security, rather than a general complaint about motivation.
Application: You need not solve the whole future at once. Identify what you need now and an available action or contact. The story is not a promise of the same outcome for every reader.
Try: Name one immediate need and contact one real source of support for it today.
