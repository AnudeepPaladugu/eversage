Soto Zen
Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Dogen learns from an older cook
Source: Tenzo Kyokun — Account of the cook drying mushrooms
Type: Monastic teaching narrative
Situation: The work in front of me feels too ordinary to matter.
What happened: Dogen sees an older monastery cook drying mushrooms in the heat. When he asks why another person cannot do it or why it cannot wait, the cook points him back to this person’s responsibility and this time for doing it.
Teaching: The encounter challenges the idea that ordinary work is separate from practice.
Why it fits: This fits dismissing everyday work, not the need for medical rest or relief from overload.
Application: This is not advice to ignore age, heat or health. The useful question is whether you are dismissing a necessary act only because it is not impressive.
Try: Choose one overlooked task and name the person or purpose it serves before starting.

The moving shore changes when attention changes
Source: Shobogenzo — Genjokoan, boat-and-shore illustration
Type: Philosophical illustration
Situation: I am treating my first view of the situation as the whole truth.
What happened: Dogen describes a person in a boat who looks at the shore and may think the shore is moving. Looking closely at the boat changes the understanding. The illustration appears in a discussion of self and experience.
Teaching: The image asks the reader to examine the standpoint from which an impression is formed.
Why it fits: This fits a fixed interpretation or comparison, not evidence that every problem is imaginary.
Application: Before accepting a painful comparison or assumption, ask what your view leaves out. Another standpoint may reveal something important without making your feelings unreal.
Try: Write your first conclusion and one fact you would need to check before treating it as certain.
