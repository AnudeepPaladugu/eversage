Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Epictetus prepares a student for the bathhouse
Source: Enchiridion — Section 4
Type: Philosophical teaching
Situation: Small predictable frustrations keep ruining my day.
What happened: Epictetus asks a person planning a bath to remember what can happen there: splashing, crowding and rude behaviour. He links the plan to bathe with the intention to keep one’s conduct steady.
Teaching: The teaching prepares for friction before it arrives.
Why it fits: This fits recurring minor friction, not serious harm that requires protection.
Application: If the same setting repeatedly frustrates you, decide in advance how you want to respond. This does not mean giving up action when a problem can be fixed.
Try: Name one predictable irritation and prepare a brief response or exit plan before entering the situation.

A brother’s action is not the only handle
Source: Enchiridion — Section 43
Type: Philosophical illustration
Situation: One painful incident has become the only way I see a family member.
What happened: Epictetus describes an affair as having two handles and gives the example of a brother behaving wrongly. He contrasts holding only the wrong with also considering the relationship.
Teaching: The illustration asks how the way one holds an event affects the response.
Why it fits: This fits a narrowed view of a family conflict, not a requirement to reconcile.
Application: You can consider a wider picture without excusing the act. In an unsafe relationship, distance can still be necessary.
Try: Separate the action you object to from the whole history. Decide which boundary or conversation is appropriate now.

Marcus writes to himself when getting up feels difficult
Source: Meditations — Book 5, section 1
Type: Personal philosophical notes
Situation: I keep putting off work that I know matters.
What happened: Marcus Aurelius writes a morning reminder to himself about getting up for human work. He weighs the appeal of comfort against the activity appropriate to human life.
Teaching: The passage is a personal prompt toward meaningful activity, not a diagnosis of anyone who cannot get out of bed.
Why it fits: This fits avoidant delay when you have capacity, not a reason to shame yourself for being unwell.
Application: For ordinary avoidance, reconnect the task with the person or purpose it serves. Exhaustion, illness and depression may call for a different kind of care.
Try: Name why the task matters and begin with five manageable minutes.
