Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Zarathushtra asks where he can find support
Source: Avesta — Yasna 46.1–2
Type: Scriptural hymn
Situation: I feel rejected by my community and do not know where to turn.
What happened: The speaker, understood in the tradition as Zarathushtra, asks where he should go when excluded from family and community. He names his lack of resources and turns to Ahura Mazda for support.
Teaching: The hymn voices need and rejection directly instead of disguising them as strength.
Why it fits: This fits exclusion and a lack of support, not a random career problem.
Application: Feeling unsupported is a reason to look for real support, not a final verdict on your worth. Faith and a practical search for allies can be held together.
Try: Name one safe person or community outside the rejecting circle whom you can approach.

A hymn asks each listener to choose thoughtfully
Source: Avesta — Yasna 30.2–3
Type: Scriptural teaching
Situation: I am being pushed to choose before I have thought clearly.
What happened: The hymn calls its listeners to hear and consider, then choose with understanding. The surrounding verses contrast better and harmful ways of thought, word and deed.
Teaching: Choice is linked to discernment and ethical responsibility.
Why it fits: This fits a pressured ethical decision, not a promise that certainty will be complete.
Application: You can ask for the facts and the time needed for a serious decision. A considered answer need not be the loudest or fastest one.
Try: Write what you know, what you need to find out and which value the choice should protect.
