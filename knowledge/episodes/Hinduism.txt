Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Rama loses the future he expected
Source: Valmiki Ramayana — Ayodhya Kanda, Sargas 18–19
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: My job ended and my plans have fallen apart.
What happened: Rama is preparing for coronation. Kaikeyi instead tells him that Bharata will rule and he must spend fourteen years in the forest. Rama is pained, but prepares to leave rather than turn his disappointment into a fight for the throne.
Teaching: The episode shows a sudden loss of position without a loss of the ability to choose one’s conduct.
Why it fits: The shared difficulty is a future changing suddenly, not the details of royal exile.
Application: Losing a role can shake your identity. You can seek fair treatment and still decide what needs your care next. Rama’s response is an example, not a rule that you must accept injustice.
Try: Name the most urgent change this loss creates. Choose one person who can help you deal with that change today.

Hanuman is reminded of his strength
Source: Valmiki Ramayana — Kishkindha Kanda, Sargas 66–67
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: I do not think I can handle this responsibility.
What happened: The search party must cross the ocean to reach Lanka. Jambavan speaks to Hanuman about his strength and ability. With that encouragement, Hanuman declares what he can do and prepares for the leap.
Teaching: Encouragement is connected to a real task and real abilities, not empty praise.
Why it fits: This fits doubt about a demanding task, rather than grief or a broken relationship.
Application: Instead of asking whether you are capable of everything, look at the skills this task actually needs. A trusted person may help you see evidence you are overlooking.
Try: Ask someone who knows your work to name one strength you can use. Then choose the first part of the task.

Krishna welcomes Sudama with honour
Source: Bhagavata Purana — Canto 10, Chapters 80–81
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: I feel too unsuccessful to meet an old friend.
What happened: Sudama arrives at Krishna’s palace in poverty, carrying a small gift of rice. Krishna welcomes his old friend warmly and personally attends to him. Sudama’s poor clothing does not decide the respect he receives.
Teaching: The friendship is presented through affection and hospitality rather than social rank.
Why it fits: The shared issue is feeling lesser because another person appears more successful.
Application: If shame about money or status is making you hide, notice that it is a fear about how you will be received, not proof that you have no place in the friendship.
Try: Send a simple message to a trusted old friend without apologising for your present status.

Arjuna admits that he cannot decide
Source: Bhagavad Gita — 1:28–47; 2:7–10; 18:63
Type: Teaching dialogue
Situation: Two important duties pull me in different directions.
What happened: Seeing relatives and teachers on the battlefield, Arjuna becomes distressed and puts down his bow. He tells Krishna he is confused about duty and asks for guidance. Later, Krishna asks him to reflect on the teaching and then choose.
Teaching: The dialogue begins with a clearly named moral conflict and a request for counsel, not a demand to stop feeling.
Why it fits: This fits conflict between values, not every situation involving work.
Application: A career or family decision is not Arjuna’s battle. The useful method is to name the values in conflict, seek thoughtful counsel and make a considered choice.
Try: Write the two values you are trying to protect. Ask a trusted adviser to help you examine the trade-off.

Dhruva turns rejection into a search
Source: Bhagavata Purana — Canto 4, Chapter 8
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: Someone in my family makes me feel that I do not belong.
What happened: Dhruva is hurt when his stepmother rejects his attempt to approach his father. After speaking with his mother, he leaves on a spiritual search and receives guidance from Narada. His hurt becomes the starting point of a deeper search.
Teaching: The story takes the child’s hurt seriously and introduces guidance rather than leaving him alone with it.
Why it fits: This fits being made to feel lesser within a family.
Application: Another person’s rejection is not a complete measure of your worth. The modern application is to seek safe support, not to copy Dhruva’s departure or severe spiritual practices.
Try: Tell a safe, trusted person what was said and how it affected you. Ask for the support you need.

Rama grieves when he hears of his father’s death
Source: Valmiki Ramayana — Ayodhya Kanda, Sargas 102–103
Type: Scripture narrative
Situation: I feel weak because I cannot stop grieving.
What happened: Bharata tells Rama of Dasharatha’s death. Rama is overcome by the news, grieves with those around him and performs the water offering for his father. The story does not present him as untouched by loss.
Teaching: The episode gives grief, family and remembrance a place even in the life of Rama.
Why it fits: This fits mourning a death, rather than treating every disappointment as the same loss.
Application: Your grief does not make you less worthy or less faithful. You can let others stand beside you and choose a form of remembrance that fits your beliefs.
Try: Ask one trusted person to share a memory or sit with you without trying to fix the feeling.
