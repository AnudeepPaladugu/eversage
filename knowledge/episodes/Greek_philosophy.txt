Greek philosophy
Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

Socrates chooses plain speech before the court
Source: Apology — 17a–18a
Type: Philosophical account
Situation: I am afraid I must sound impressive to be taken seriously.
What happened: At the opening of Plato’s account, Socrates distinguishes persuasive display from speaking truthfully. He asks the court to attend to whether what he says is just, rather than the polish of his language.
Teaching: The scene contrasts an account’s substance with its presentation.
Why it fits: This fits fear about how you sound in a serious conversation.
Application: Preparation matters, but you need not copy an impressive persona to explain what you know. The modern parallel is limited; an interview is not Socrates’ trial.
Try: Prepare your main point and one clear example before polishing the wording.

Socrates asks Crito to examine the reason for escape
Source: Crito — 46b–49e
Type: Philosophical dialogue
Situation: People are urging me to act, but I am unsure the action is right.
What happened: Crito urges Socrates to escape from prison. Socrates asks them to examine the argument rather than decide from fear of public opinion, and discusses whether one should answer wrongdoing with wrongdoing.
Teaching: The dialogue makes the reasons for a choice central even under pressure.
Why it fits: This fits pressure to act against a considered value.
Application: You do not need to adopt every conclusion of the dialogue. Its useful method is to test the proposed action against reasons you can defend.
Try: Write the strongest reason for the action and the strongest ethical objection to it before deciding.
