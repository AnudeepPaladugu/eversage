Bahá’í Faith
Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

The frightened lover sees only part of the path
Source: The Seven Valleys — Valley of Knowledge, lover and watchman story
Type: Spiritual parable
Situation: An obstacle has made me feel that there is no way forward.
What happened: A lover searching for his beloved is pursued by a watchman. Trying to escape, he climbs a wall into a garden and finds the person he sought. The story is used to explore the limits of a narrow view of events.
Teaching: The parable invites a wider perspective; it does not say readers can already know the purpose of every painful event.
Why it fits: This fits feeling that one blocked route means every route is closed.
Application: You need not call the obstacle good. You can still ask whether another route or a piece of information is missing.
Try: List one route that is blocked and two alternatives you have not yet checked.

Justice asks for your own careful seeing
Source: The Hidden Words — Arabic, number 2
Type: Scriptural teaching
Situation: Other people’s opinions are deciding how I see myself.
What happened: This passage praises justice and asks the reader to see with their own eyes and know through their own understanding, rather than depending entirely on another person’s view.
Teaching: Its setting is a teaching about justice and discernment, not a promise of social approval.
Why it fits: This fits borrowed judgments and comparison, rather than every form of sadness.
Application: Separate what you actually know from what others have assumed. A fair view can include both your effort and what still needs work.
Try: Write one judgment you have accepted and the evidence that supports or challenges it.
