Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

The gods work together outside Amaterasu’s cave
Source: Kojiki — Book I, the Heavenly Rock Cave episode
Type: Sacred narrative
Situation: After a painful conflict, I want to shut everyone out.
What happened: After Susanoo’s disruptive actions, Amaterasu withdraws into the rock cave and the world loses her light. The other deities gather and work out a way to draw her attention outward, with ritual, laughter and a mirror.
Teaching: The episode is a sacred account of withdrawal and a collective response, not a clinical account of depression.
Why it fits: This fits withdrawal after hurt; it does not mean you owe others a cheerful performance.
Application: After conflict, distance may protect you. When you are ready, one safe connection may be a gentler step than demanding a full return to normal.
Try: Choose one safe person and one small form of contact that you can accept without pressure.

Okuninushi asks the injured hare what happened
Source: Kojiki — Book I, the White Hare of Inaba episode
Type: Sacred narrative
Situation: My advice is not helping a friend who is hurting.
What happened: The hare has received advice that worsens its condition. Okuninushi encounters it, asks why it is suffering, hears its account and responds differently. The story contrasts careless treatment with attentive help.
Teaching: The narrative’s treatment details are not medical instructions for a reader.
Why it fits: This fits help that has become advice without understanding.
Application: The useful parallel is to learn what happened before repeating familiar advice. Ask what your friend needs and recognise when skilled help is needed.
Try: Ask what has already been tried and whether your friend wants listening, practical help or help finding an appropriate professional.
