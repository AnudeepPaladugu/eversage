Mahayana Buddhism
Source-based retellings; original applications are not scripture quotations.

The son is helped to grow into what he cannot yet believe
Source: Lotus Sutra — Chapter 4, Faith and Understanding
Type: Scriptural parable
Situation: An opportunity feels too big and I do not believe I belong there.
What happened: In the parable, a poor son encounters his wealthy father without recognising the relationship. He is frightened by what seems beyond him. The father approaches gradually, allowing familiarity and confidence to develop before disclosing the inheritance.
Teaching: The parable explains gradual teaching in a Buddhist setting, not material wealth for every believer.
Why it fits: This fits feeling unready for an unfamiliar opportunity.
Application: An unfamiliar role may need a supported beginning. You can ask for stages, explanation and practice rather than deciding immediately that you do not belong.
Try: Ask for one smaller responsibility through which you can learn the larger role.

Never Disparaging keeps respect without staying within reach of harm
Source: Lotus Sutra — Chapter 20, Bodhisattva Never Disparaging
Type: Scriptural narrative
Situation: People mock me and I am starting to lose respect for myself.
What happened: The bodhisattva Never Disparaging expresses respect for others’ potential. Some respond with hostility. The narrative describes him withdrawing to a distance while continuing his respectful message.
Teaching: Respect is paired with movement away from immediate harm.
Why it fits: This fits contempt and ridicule, rather than an ordinary difference of opinion.
Application: You can refuse to make cruelty your measure of worth and still take practical distance. The story does not ask you to remain available to mistreatment.
Try: Choose one boundary or source of support that will reduce your exposure to the mockery.
